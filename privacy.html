Privacy policy

Last updated 18 November 2026. This policy covers the Earshot app for Android (package uk.jj.earshot) and this website. Earshot is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Earshot has no accounts and no servers of its own. The developer receives nothing from your phone.
  • There is no advertising and no analytics in the app.
  • Your podcasts, your place in each episode, your queue, your bookmarks and your settings are kept on your phone.
  • To play or download an episode, your phone connects straight to that podcast’s host. To show charts and search results, it asks a public directory. Those services see what any website sees: your internet address and what was asked for. They are listed below.
  • Earshot does not use your location, your contacts, your microphone or your camera.

What stays on your phone

Earshot keeps the following in its private storage:

  • The podcasts you have added, with their episode lists and the choices you made for each: its speed, what to skip, its order, and whether to tell you about new episodes or download them.
  • Your place in each episode, which episodes you have finished, and when you last listened.
  • Your queue and your bookmarks, with their notes.
  • Episodes you have downloaded. These are sound files in the app’s own folder and are removed if you uninstall Earshot.
  • Your settings.
  • The built-in list of podcasts, which comes with the app, and a temporary copy of the show notes of podcasts you have opened.

Unless you switch it off, Earshot also keeps one backup copy of your podcasts, places, queue, bookmarks and settings in the phone’s Documents folder, in a folder called Earshot. That file stays there if you uninstall the app, so that you can restore from it. If your phone’s own backup to your Google account is on, Android may include Earshot’s settings and podcasts in it, as it does for most apps.

Services the app contacts

Earshot has no server of its own. Your phone connects directly to the services below. Each sees your internet address and the technical details any web server sees, and handles them under its own privacy policy. None of them is sent your name, your list of podcasts or your listening history, because Earshot does not have a way to send them.

Downloads are carried out by Android’s own download manager, which shows them in the phone’s notifications. Earshot allows plain, unencrypted connections, because some podcasts still use them; on such a connection your network provider can see which podcast you are fetching.

Podcasts

Earshot does not make, host or change any podcast. What you hear comes straight from its publisher, who is responsible for it and for any adverts or listener counting in it.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • A podcast is removed with the Added button on its page. Bookmarks and queued episodes can each be removed where they are listed.
  • Downloaded episodes can be deleted one at a time, or all the finished ones at once, from Library › Downloads. Earshot also deletes finished downloads by itself unless you switch that off.
  • Clearing the app’s storage or uninstalling Earshot deletes everything it holds, including downloads, apart from the backup copy.
  • The backup copy is an ordinary file, earshot-backup.json, in Documents under Earshot. Delete it there when you no longer want it. A backup file you saved yourself is wherever you put it.
  • Nothing is held by the developer, so there is nothing further to delete.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of Earshot holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. What Earshot stores is on your own phone, where you control it directly using the steps above. For data held by a podcast host or one of the services listed, contact that service. If you need help, use the contact link below. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

What Earshot stores is kept in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read. The backup copy in Documents can be read by you and by apps you have given access to your files; it holds podcast addresses, your places, your bookmark notes and settings, and no passwords. A private feed link that carries a personal code in its address is stored like any other, so treat a backup holding one as you would the link itself. Connections to the directory and to GitHub are encrypted. Connections to podcast hosts are encrypted where the host offers it.

No software is free of faults. Earshot’s source code is public so that anyone can check what it does.

This website

This site sets no cookies and has no analytics or advertising. It is hosted by GitHub Pages, and the app file is downloaded from GitHub Releases. GitHub, as the host, receives the technical details any web server sees, such as your IP address and browser type, and handles them under the GitHub privacy statement. The developer does not receive them.

Children

Earshot is not aimed at children and collects nothing from anyone. Podcasts carry all kinds of material, some of it unsuitable for children. A parent or guardian should decide whether a child uses it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it and the new version is published here.

Contact

For a question about privacy or your data, open an issue on GitHub. Please do not put private details in an issue; it is public.
